One of the hardest things I contend with on a day-to-day basis is the right way to spend my time. In fact, I’d say it’s been my one true burden–or purpose, depending on how you look at things. In the hero’s journey of my life, I am questing to defeat what Thomas C. Oden calls “demonic boredom.”

To that end, I have accrued a great deal of hobbies. Over the years, I’ve learned three languages (Spanish, Portuguese, and German), played two sports (volleyball and swim), and picked up a number of other things to fill my time. I like logic games like Sudoku, Pyralinks , and whatever new New York Times mini is popular. I enjoy cozy hobbies like puzzles and reading. I practice yoga and hike when the weather is nice. I study art history, fashion history, world history, and visit museums and libraries when specific exhibitions pique my interest.

Between the end of my most recent job and the beginning of my Master’s program, I had a lot of time to contend with feeling bored. While filling my time brought me a great sense of purpose in the interim, I also came to realize the importance of valuing doing nothing. It is a great privilege to experience “boredom,” and I have tried to learn to disentangle work, school, and productivity in general from purpose and happiness.
